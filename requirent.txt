# Build an Open-World Infinite Driving Game

You are the lead game developer and technical architect.

Build a browser-based 3D driving game inspired by the relaxing endless-driving concept of Slow Roads, but **do not copy its code, assets, branding, UI, or distinctive visual identity**.

The goal is to create a much more ambitious experience:

> **An infinite procedural road-trip game where the player can drive continuously through natural landscapes, rural areas, towns, suburbs, and large cities without loading screens or reaching a fixed world boundary.**

The game should feel like one enormous continuously generated world.

---

# 1. CORE EXPERIENCE

The player controls a car from a third-person driving camera.

The world continuously generates around the player as they drive.

The player should be able to:

- Drive indefinitely.
- Travel through forests.
- Drive through mountains.
- Cross plains and valleys.
- Pass farms and rural buildings.
- Enter villages.
- Enter towns.
- Transition into suburbs.
- Enter dense cities.
- Leave cities and return to countryside.
- Encounter rivers, bridges, tunnels, coastlines, lakes and other landmarks.
- Follow roads that curve, climb, descend and branch.
- Take turns and choose different routes.
- Explore rather than simply follow one endlessly repeating road.

There should be **no traditional level boundary**.

The world should feel procedurally generated and effectively infinite.

---

# 2. IMPORTANT DESIGN GOAL

Do NOT create a simple straight endless road.

The world needs a proper procedural road network.

The player should encounter:

- Main highways
- Country roads
- Mountain roads
- City streets
- Residential streets
- Industrial roads
- Downtown streets
- Ring roads
- Bridges
- Intersections
- Roundabouts
- T-junctions
- Four-way intersections
- Highway exits
- Merging roads
- Forks
- Dead-end optional roads
- Scenic routes

The player should be able to make meaningful driving choices.

For example:

COUNTRYSIDE
↓
SMALL TOWN
↓
HIGHWAY
↓
SUBURBS
↓
CITY
↓
CITY OUTSKIRTS
↓
MOUNTAINS
↓
FOREST
↓
COAST
↓
ANOTHER CITY

This transition should happen naturally.

---

# 3. PROCEDURAL WORLD

Create a procedural world-generation system.

The world should be generated from a deterministic seed.

Given the same seed and player position, the same environment should be regenerated.

Use chunk-based generation.

For example:

World
├── Chunk
├── Chunk
├── Chunk
├── Chunk
└── ...

Only generate chunks close enough to the player to be visible/useful.

Unload distant chunks to keep memory usage low.

The system must support:

- World coordinates
- Chunk coordinates
- Deterministic generation
- Seeded randomness
- Procedural terrain
- Procedural roads
- Procedural vegetation
- Procedural buildings
- Procedural cities
- Procedural landmarks

The player should never see an obvious "chunk loading wall."

---

# 4. SEAMLESS WORLD STREAMING

The player should be able to drive continuously without:

- Loading screens
- Teleportation
- Visible chunk popping
- Abrupt biome changes
- Roads suddenly ending
- Buildings appearing directly in front of the player

Generate upcoming terrain before the player reaches it.

Use a streaming radius around the player.

Prioritize generation in this order:

1. Road
2. Terrain
3. Nearby collision objects
4. Buildings
5. Vegetation
6. Decorative objects
7. Distant scenery

Use lower-detail representations for distant objects.

---

# 5. TERRAIN SYSTEM

Create procedural terrain with smooth elevation changes.

Terrain should support:

- Hills
- Mountains
- Valleys
- Plains
- Cliffs
- Forest regions
- Coastlines
- Lakes
- Rivers

Avoid obviously repetitive noise.

Use layered procedural noise or another suitable procedural terrain technique.

Roads must conform naturally to terrain.

Avoid roads floating above terrain or clipping through hills.

---

# 6. BIOME SYSTEM

Create a biome system.

At minimum support:

### Forest

Dense trees, grass, dirt paths, hills.

### Countryside

Fields, farms, fences, barns and scattered houses.

### Mountain

Steep terrain, cliffs, rock formations, sparse vegetation and winding roads.

### Coastal

Ocean, beaches, cliffs and coastal roads.

### Village

Small houses, narrow roads and modest density.

### Town

More buildings, intersections, shops and traffic.

### Suburban

Houses, sidewalks, driveways, trees and local roads.

### City

Dense buildings, multiple lanes, intersections, traffic, sidewalks, streetlights and larger structures.

### Downtown

High-density buildings, major intersections, urban lighting and significant traffic.

Biomes must transition gradually rather than suddenly switching.

---

# 7. CITY GENERATION

Cities are a major feature.

Do not simply place random buildings beside the road.

Generate coherent city layouts.

A city should have:

- Districts
- Main roads
- Side streets
- Residential areas
- Commercial areas
- Industrial areas
- Downtown
- Parks
- Parking areas
- Intersections
- Traffic lights
- Sidewalks
- Buildings with varying heights

Cities should have recognizable structure.

Example:

OUTER CITY
→ SUBURBS
→ COMMERCIAL DISTRICT
→ DENSE RESIDENTIAL
→ DOWNTOWN
→ INDUSTRIAL AREA
→ OUTER CITY

Cities should be procedurally generated from the world seed.

---

# 8. CITY-TO-COUNTRYSIDE TRANSITIONS

This is one of the most important features.

Do not make:

COUNTRY → CITY WALL

Instead create a transition:

FOREST
→ scattered houses
→ farms
→ village
→ small town
→ suburban houses
→ commercial buildings
→ multi-lane roads
→ dense city

Leaving a city should work in reverse.

This should feel like a genuine road trip.

---

# 9. ROAD NETWORK

Create a procedural graph-based road system.

Roads should be represented as connected nodes and segments.

Each road segment should know:

- Start point
- End point
- Width
- Number of lanes
- Road type
- Speed category
- Terrain relationship
- Connected roads

Use different road-generation rules for:

- Rural roads
- Highways
- Town roads
- Residential roads
- City roads

Roads should have smooth curves.

Avoid sharp unnatural turns unless intentionally generated.

---

# 10. INTERSECTIONS

Generate actual intersections.

Support:

- T intersections
- 4-way intersections
- Roundabouts
- Highway merges
- Highway exits
- Forks
- Crossroads

Intersections must connect correctly.

No floating roads.

No roads ending in buildings.

No impossible road geometry.

---

# 11. DRIVING

Create satisfying arcade-style driving.

The car should have:

- Acceleration
- Braking
- Steering
- Reverse
- Friction
- Grip
- Drifting/sliding at appropriate speeds
- Gravity
- Suspension movement

The handling should be accessible and relaxing rather than a hardcore simulator.

Keyboard controls:

W / Arrow Up = Accelerate

S / Arrow Down = Brake / Reverse

A / Arrow Left = Steer Left

D / Arrow Right = Steer Right

Space = Handbrake

R = Reset vehicle

Add gamepad support if practical.

---

# 12. CAMERA

Use a smooth third-person chase camera.

Camera should:

- Follow the vehicle smoothly.
- Look toward the direction of travel.
- Slightly lag behind steering.
- Adjust distance based on speed.
- Avoid clipping through terrain/buildings.
- Provide a cinematic feeling.

Allow optional camera modes:

1. Chase
2. Close Chase
3. Hood
4. First Person

---

# 13. TRAFFIC

Add procedural AI traffic.

Vehicles should:

- Follow roads.
- Stay in lanes.
- Slow down.
- Stop at intersections.
- React to other vehicles.
- Turn at intersections.
- Enter and leave highways.
- Spawn/despawn intelligently.

Traffic density should depend on environment.

Example:

Forest:
Very little traffic.

Rural:
Low traffic.

Town:
Moderate traffic.

Suburb:
Moderate traffic.

City:
High traffic.

Downtown:
Very high traffic.

Do not spawn traffic directly in front of the player.

---

# 14. BUILDINGS

Create procedural building generation.

Buildings should vary in:

- Height
- Width
- Shape
- Color/material
- Windows
- Roof style

Use different building generators for:

- Farms
- Rural houses
- Village houses
- Suburban houses
- Shops
- Offices
- Apartments
- Industrial buildings
- Downtown skyscrapers

Use instancing and LOD to keep performance reasonable.

---

# 15. VEGETATION

Generate procedural:

- Trees
- Bushes
- Grass
- Rocks
- Flowers
- Crops

Use instancing wherever possible.

Do not create thousands of expensive individual objects.

---

# 16. LANDMARKS

Generate occasional memorable landmarks.

Examples:

- Large bridge
- Mountain pass
- Wind farm
- Radio tower
- Lighthouse
- Dam
- Lake
- Large stadium
- Factory
- Airport
- Monument
- Tunnel
- Coastal viewpoint

Landmarks should be relatively rare.

They should help the world feel like a real journey instead of pure procedural noise.

---

# 17. TIME OF DAY

Add a dynamic time system.

The world should transition smoothly through:

Morning
→ Day
→ Evening
→ Sunset
→ Night
→ Sunrise

Lighting should change naturally.

Add:

- Sun movement
- Sky color changes
- Night lighting
- Building lights
- Street lights
- Vehicle headlights

Allow the player to optionally control time or use real-time progression.

---

# 18. WEATHER

Implement a scalable weather system.

At minimum:

- Clear
- Cloudy
- Rain
- Fog

Optional later:

- Heavy rain
- Storm
- Snow

Weather should affect atmosphere.

Rain can optionally affect vehicle grip slightly.

Do not make weather overly distracting.

---

# 19. AUDIO

Create immersive ambient audio.

Include:

- Engine sound
- Tire sounds
- Wind
- Rain
- Forest ambience
- City ambience
- Traffic
- Birds
- Water
- Tunnel reverb

Audio should change depending on environment.

Example:

Forest → birds + wind

City → traffic + distant urban ambience

Tunnel → engine echo

Coast → wind + waves

---

# 20. GRAPHICS

Target a polished stylized 3D aesthetic.

The game should look impressive while remaining browser-friendly.

Prioritize:

- Good lighting
- Atmospheric perspective
- Procedural detail
- Smooth terrain
- Attractive roads
- Strong environmental variety

Do not prioritize extremely high-poly assets.

Performance matters more than raw polygon count.

---

# 21. WEB TECHNOLOGY

Build this as a browser game.

Preferred technology:

- TypeScript
- Three.js
- WebGL/WebGPU where appropriate
- Vite

Use a clean modular architecture.

Suggested structure:

src/
  core/
  world/
  terrain/
  roads/
  cities/
  biomes/
  vehicles/
  traffic/
  buildings/
  vegetation/
  weather/
  audio/
  camera/
  ui/
  rendering/
  utils/

Keep systems modular.

Do not put the entire game into one giant file.

---

# 22. PERFORMANCE

Performance is extremely important.

Target:

60 FPS on a reasonably modern desktop/laptop.

Use:

- Object instancing
- LOD
- Frustum culling
- Distance-based rendering
- Chunk streaming
- Object pooling
- Texture atlases where useful
- Simplified distant geometry
- Efficient collision detection

Do not create unnecessary physics objects.

Traffic AI should only run at full detail near the player.

Distant traffic can use simplified simulation.

---

# 23. INFINITE WORLD TECHNIQUE

The world should effectively be infinite.

Avoid allowing floating-point precision problems during extremely long journeys.

Consider a floating-origin/re-centering system.

The player should be able to drive extremely far without the world becoming unstable.

The world seed and logical coordinates must remain consistent even when rendering coordinates are shifted.

---

Create a minimal modern UI.

Main menu:

PLAY
SETTINGS
CONTROLS
ABOUT

In-game HUD should be minimal.

Show optionally:

- Speed
- Distance traveled
- Current biome
- Time
- Weather

Avoid clutter.

The game should primarily feel like an exploration experience.

Track:

- Landmarks discovered

"First City"

---

# 26. WORLD CONSISTENCY

This is extremely important.

If the player turns around and drives back, the world should still be recognizable.

Do NOT regenerate completely different terrain every time a chunk is loaded.

The procedural seed must guarantee deterministic results.

Road networks must remain consistent.

Cities must remain consistent.

Landmarks must remain consistent.

---

# 27. NO FAKE INFINITE LOOP

Do not implement the world by secretly looping the same terrain/road.

The player should be able to travel in different directions and discover different procedural environments.

The system should generate a genuinely large procedural world.

---

---

# 29. IMPORTANT AGENT BEHAVIOR

You are an autonomous senior developer.

Before implementing a system, inspect the existing project.

Do not unnecessarily rewrite working code.

Keep the architecture maintainable.

When you encounter a difficult technical problem, solve it systematically rather than replacing the feature with a fake approximation.

Do not create placeholder systems and then declare the feature complete.

If a feature cannot reasonably be implemented yet, clearly identify it as incomplete.

After every major implementation:

1. Run the application.
2. Check for TypeScript errors.
3. Check browser console errors.
4. Test the feature.
5. Fix problems before moving on.

Do not leave obvious errors behind.

---

# 30. PRIORITY ORDER

When tradeoffs are necessary, prioritize:

1. Driving feel
2. Seamless world streaming
3. Road generation
4. World variety
5. City transitions
6. Performance
7. Traffic
8. Visual polish
9. Weather
10. Extra features

A smaller world that feels seamless is better than a visually impressive world that constantly breaks.

---

# 31. FINAL EXPERIENCE TARGET

The final experience should feel like:

> "I can get into my car, choose a direction, and just drive."

The player should be able to start in a quiet forest road, drive for a while, discover a small village, continue onto a highway, see a city skyline in the distance, enter the suburbs, drive through downtown, leave the city on another road, climb through mountains, reach the coast, and continue indefinitely.

There should be no obvious moment where the player thinks:

"Now the game has generated a new level."

It should feel like one continuous world.

Build the foundation properly first. Do not rush to add superficial features before the procedural world, road network, streaming system, and driving are solid.
